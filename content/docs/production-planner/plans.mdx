Once a plan exists, refine it until it matches the factory you want to build.

## Desired outputs

Open the **Outputs** panel to add or remove target items and change their
amounts. The whole plan recalculates automatically.

## Recipes & alternates

Each step shows the recipe and building it uses. Open **Summary → Plan
validation → Recipe routes** to inspect up to ten complete routes for each
produced item. The planner selects the best route for your current strategy,
but you can choose another route or return to **Automatic (best route)**. Each
option compares route depth, raw-input score, and byproducts.

## Catalogs & availability

The creation wizard and the in-planner **Settings** panel use searchable
catalog dialogs instead of long select lists:

- The **Recipe Catalog** filters by name, input, output, building, category,
  base/alternate recipe, unlock state, and availability. Use compact or
  detailed rows, enable recipes individually, or apply **All**, **None**,
  **Base only**, or **Unlocked only** in one action. Detailed rows also expose
  a recipe contribution cap when you need to limit how much of an item that
  route may produce.
- The **Building Catalog** shows each supported producer with its image and
  recipe count. Search the list or enable and disable producers individually
  or in bulk.
- The **Raw Resource Catalog** in the creation wizard groups genuine
  mineable/extractable resources by kind. Disabling one removes every route
  that depends on it; ordinary producerless items are never silently treated
  as raw resources.

Catalog rows explain why an option is unavailable. A recipe explicitly chosen
as a target or final route is marked **Required** and cannot itself be disabled.
The Review step still warns when its required building or raw input has been
disabled, so the effect is visible before calculation.

In **Settings → Availability & unlocks**, import a Satisfactory save to limit
recipes and buildings to what that save has unlocked. The import summary shows
what was detected; you can clear it without changing the save file.

If no permitted dependency route remains, the planner reports the unresolved
item instead of silently using an excluded resource or invalid fallback.

The resolver only accepts a mineable resource or an item tagged
`KMods.PlannerResource` as a final external input. A normal item with no
producer is never silently treated as raw: calculation marks the plan invalid
and explains the unresolved item or cycle instead.

The recipe catalog is stored as a versioned item/recipe graph. Candidate
routes are only offered when their complete dependency chain reaches an
allowed raw resource or one of your Available Inputs. Calculating and balancing
those routes runs in a bounded backend worker pool, so a large plan does not
block unrelated API requests.

## Miners

Open **Configure Miner** from the Outputs panel and set the resource, node
**purity**, **miner tier**, and **overclock**. Not sure what overclock to
use? Enter a **target rate** instead and it calculates the overclock
percentage needed to hit it. Your configured miners show up under
**Configured Miners** in the same panel, and the calculator applies each one
directly wherever the plan needs that resource no manual available input
needed, and no fixed rate: the miner scales with the rest of the plan just
like a recipe node does, with its own output, byproduct, and any required
fluid input wired in as real connections.

A resource deposit is treated as the miner's extraction source, not as a
material that the miner consumes and reproduces. This matters for Fluid
Modules whose output is still the same resource: their required Water or other
fluid remains part of the material balance instead of being optimized away.
Waste Module replacement outputs and byproducts likewise remain real outputs
and can feed another recipe or appear as surplus.

For the Modular Miner, some resources also let you attach modules. The
**Waste Module** (Crusher or Smelter, pick one) replaces the raw ore with a
crushed intermediate or a primary ingot at the same total rate, plus a small
byproduct. The **Fluid Module** boosts the extraction rate using a supplied
fluid (Water, Muriatic Acid, etc.) some resources (Sulfur, for example) can
only be extracted with it attached at all. The two modules sit on
independent attachment slots in-game, so you can combine a Waste Module with
a Fluid Module on the same miner for both effects at once.

**Power Modules** come in a `+50%` and a `+25%` version, and you can attach up
to three of each at the same time. Like the Fluid Module's boost, their
percentages add together rather than multiplying, so three of each gives
`+225%` extraction. A Crusher or Smelter dampens that combined boost the same
way it dampens the Fluid Module's (a Crusher halves it, a Smelter cancels it).

Overclock runs from `1%` to `100%`. Miners use the game's own extractor power
curve, so their power draw follows the clock you set.

Once a miner is dialed in, **Copy settings to another resource** applies the
same head tier, purity, overclock, and Power modules to a second resource in
one click handy when a whole set of nodes shares the same setup. Waste and
Fluid modules belong to a specific resource, so they are re-picked for the
target: the same kind of module if it has one, otherwise none (or its required
Fluid module, if it can only be extracted with one).

## Slug Hatchery

Open **Hatchery Info** from the Outputs panel and pick a slug color to see
the heat and humidity range it needs to hatch or breed, whether a Tank
module supplying a fluid is required, the Incubator tier, and which modules
to build. The mod treats hatching as something you test for in-game, so
these are target ranges to dial modules toward, not exact settings.

Any recipe elsewhere in your plan that consumes a slug directly also shows
that slug's conditions right on its own node, so you don't need to look it
up separately.

:::note
Purity comes from the actual node in the game world (impure/normal/pure) it's
not something you choose freely, so set it to match the node you actually plan
to build on.
:::

## Notes

Drop a sticky **note** anywhere to label parts of your factory. Double-click a
note to edit it, and recolor it if you like.

## Sub-assemblies

Select a few related blocks and right-click to **group** them into one tidy
block. You can collapse or expand this block, which keeps large plans
readable.

## Planning strategies

Under **Settings → Optimization & byproducts** you can control alternate
recipes, overclocking, compact recipes, circular-route avoidance, and what to
do with surplus. Route priorities include depth, raw resources, power,
balance, and byproducts; select up to two in the creation wizard. Automatic
selection ranks the complete dependency route, not just the final recipe, and
remains deterministic when scores tie. A manually selected route stays
selected while the other ranked options remain available. Identical recipe
executions are aggregated only after their complete material flows have been
resolved. **Settings → Logistics & display** contains game multipliers, rate
precision, and connection layout. Some advanced strategies are **Patreon**
features. See the
[Patreon page](/production-planner/patreon) for details.

## Surplus and the AWESOME Sink

Under **Settings → Optimization & byproducts**, choose what the planner does
after it has satisfied every desired output:

- **Preserve** keeps every unused output in the plan.
- **Sink surplus** sends all eligible sinkable surplus to the AWESOME Sink.
- **Maximize Sink Points** prefers production bypasses that turn otherwise
  unused capacity into valuable sinkable items. It does not increase raw
  resource inputs merely to manufacture points; the requested factory's raw
  input budget remains the limit.

Open **Configure items** to search the sinkable item catalog. Enable only the
items the sink may accept, give preferred items a higher priority, or reserve a
rate per minute that must remain available instead of being sunk.

Every sunk item is represented by its own AWESOME Sink building card, flow,
and incoming amount in both Flow and Text views. Different items are never
merged into one sink card. Fluids and gases cannot enter the AWESOME Sink; when
the catalog provides a real disposal recipe with no output (or only a `None`
placeholder output), the planner uses that recipe as a required terminal step
instead of leaving the material as free surplus.

Raw resources resolve through real extraction routes where the catalog
provides them. Ores use the Modular Miner, while Oil, Air, and similar resources
use their matching extractor or collector. These extraction rates are included
in the Text view and exported text's **Raw resources** section, not just raw
materials imported as external inputs.
