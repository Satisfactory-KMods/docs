The canvas is your workspace. Everything you build lives here as draggable
blocks (**nodes**) connected by resource flows (**edges**).

## Moving around

Drag the empty background to pan, scroll to zoom. You can zoom out to 10% for an
overview of even very large plans. Drag a block to move it.

## Selecting & deleting

Click a block to select it. Hold and drag to box-select several blocks at once.
Press **Delete** or **Backspace** to remove the selected blocks. Connected lines
are cleaned up automatically.

## Connecting blocks

Drag from a block's output dot to another block's input dot to create a flow.

## Tracing a connection

Hover any line (or its amount label) to highlight it along with the other lines
on the same blocks. The flow then animates back toward its source, so you can
follow where a resource comes from and where it goes.

## Inspecting an ingredient

Item names on a block's input and output rows are shortened to keep blocks
compact. Hover any ingredient row to open a small item card showing its **icon**,
**full name**, and the exact throughput per minute. A solid/fluid marker is
included too, so you always know what a truncated label is.

## Finding things

Press **Ctrl+F** to search the canvas by name. Made a mistake? **Ctrl+Z** undoes
(up to 50 steps), **Ctrl+Y** redoes.

## Bottleneck warnings

If a recipe block turns red with a warning badge in its top-left corner, one or
more of its inputs aren't getting enough supply for its current building
count/clock. This usually happens because an edge was deleted or rerouted by
hand after the chain was calculated. Hover the badge to see which item(s) are
short.

:::caution
Bottleneck badges only appear after you've manually changed something (deleted
an edge, rerouted a connection, edited a building count). A freshly-generated
chain is always balanced.
:::

## Configured miner building count

The building icon in a configured raw-resource node's header shows how many
whole miners or extractors must be built. Fractional machine demand is always
rounded up; Mining Head count changes the throughput inside each miner and is
not counted as additional buildings.

## Fixed-clock recipes

Recipes that cannot be overclocked or underclocked, such as boiler and heater
combinations, always use whole buildings at 100% clock. Every building consumes
and produces its full listed rates. Extra output appears as surplus, while the
planner increases upstream production to supply every full building.

## Acting on unused output

Right-click an output row that isn't fully routed anywhere (produced but with
leftover surplus) to open a menu of actions just for that item:

- Suggest a recipe to consume it (Patreon, with a searchable list and an
  optional "auto-build chain" mode that adds several recipes deep in one
  click)
- Route it to the AWESOME Sink
- Add it as a new desired output
- Trim the node's clock/building count down to only what's actually used
- Highlight every other place that item flows through the diagram

This menu works on raw-resource and byproduct nodes too, not just recipe
blocks so a byproduct that has collected somewhere with nothing consuming
it can be dealt with right where you see it.

A suggested consuming recipe becomes a real part of the plan: the planner
recalculates with it, produces its other ingredients for you, and counts its
consumption in the material balance. It is not just a drawn-on connection.

Right-clicking anywhere on a recipe node also offers node-level actions like
duplicating it.

## Configuring a miner from the canvas

Right-click a raw-resource node, or an input row that needs a mineable
resource, and pick **Configure Miner** to open the Miner Configuration dialog
for that resource without leaving the canvas. If a configuration already
exists it opens for editing instead.

## Belt/pipe tier badges

Every connection's amount label carries a small `Mk.n` badge showing the
smallest conveyor belt or pipeline tier that can carry it alone. If the flow
is too high even for the top tier, the badge turns red and shows how many
parallel lines (e.g. `×2`) you'd need instead.

## Somersloop shards

Recipe blocks for buildings that support Somersloop production shards show a
`+`/`−` stepper (up to that building's max slots). Adding shards raises that
block's output (up to double at full slots), at the cost of extra power (up to
×4 at full amplification). The planner automatically scales the building
count/clock speed down to match, so you keep producing exactly what's routed
downstream instead of an unrouted surplus. Removing shards scales it back up
the same way.

:::note
Shards raise a building's **output-per-input** ratio, not the input rate
itself the input rate a recipe needs stays what it was; only the building
count/clock speed needed to reach the same routed output goes down.
:::

## Marking blocks as built

Each block has a check button in its top-right corner. Click it to mark that
part of your factory as built, turning the block green so you can track progress
at a glance. When both ends of a connection are built, the line and its amount
label turn green too. The state is saved with your plan.

## Connection line style

Use the line-style button in the toolbar to switch connections between 90°
(right-angle) and curved. Your choice is remembered.

## Light & dark

Toggle the theme with the button on the canvas. Your choice is remembered.
