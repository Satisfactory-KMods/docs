SF+ changes fluid-network limits, buffer pressure, and powered storage behavior. Use the
[Satisfactory Plus wiki](https://k-mods.com/wiki) or in-game build menu for building lists, unlock locations, and
current construction costs.

## Pipeline tiers

| Pipeline | Maximum flow |
| -------- | -----------: |
| Mk.1     |   150 m³/min |
| Mk.2     |   300 m³/min |
| Mk.3     |   450 m³/min |

SF+ applies the 150 m³/min limit to Mk.1 and compatible ordinary pipeline segments, then gives Mk.2 and Mk.3 their own
higher limits. A network does not inherit the best tier found anywhere in it. Every segment keeps its own limit. A short
Mk.1 section therefore remains a 150 m³/min bottleneck between Mk.2 or Mk.3 sections.

Pumps, valves, and junctions do not upgrade a pipe tier. They influence pressure, direction, or distribution while
connected pipe limits stay in force.

### Clean variants

Every tier has a standard and clean variant. Both carry the same fluids and use the same flow limit. Standard pipes show
volume, rate, and flow direction on external indicators. Clean pipes have no indicator.

A missing readout on a clean pipe is expected. Inspect a connected machine, buffer, pump, valve, or temporary standard
segment when diagnosing the line.

## Pressure, fullness, and actual flow

Rated flow sets an upper bound. Actual movement depends on supplied volume, demand, gravity, pipe fullness, and pressure
differences across the network.

Liquids need enough head lift to reach their highest point. Gases do not consume head lift while rising, but they still
need pressure to move through the network. A vertical gas line can therefore stall even though height is not consuming a
head-lift allowance.

Junctions split or merge the fluid network across their connected ports. The four-port junction tends toward an equal
split until strong pressure differences take over. Gravity, fullness, and high flow can all create those differences.
The three-port T-Junction follows the same pressure model with one fewer port.

When one branch takes more than another, check both downstream demand and the pressure on each branch. Adding another
junction does not force an equal rate.

## Pumps

Pumps are directional attachments. Placement arrows show the pressure direction. Mk.1 supplies 20 m of liquid head lift;
Mk.2 supplies 50 m.

Head lift does not add when several pumps occupy the same pressure section. Put the next pump near the point where the
previous pump's lift has been consumed. Several pumps placed together repeat pressure the line already has.

```text
source -> pump input -> pressure increase -> rising pipe -> next pressure section
                    direction matters
```

A reversed pump opposes the intended route. A correctly oriented pump can push gas along, but its head-lift rating has
no vertical meaning for gas. A pump also cannot make a 150 m³/min segment carry 300 m³/min.

When a pump appears inactive, check these in order:

1. Confirm the arrows point toward the destination.
2. Confirm fluid reaches the pump input.
3. Find any lower-tier segment after the pump.
4. Check whether the next rise exceeds remaining head lift.
5. Check whether a full consumer or byproduct line has stopped the network.

## Regular valve

The regular Valve has an input and output direction. Its interface caps flow in that direction. It does not create
pressure or head lift, so the input side still needs enough pressure to cross it.

The gauge is approximate. Compare sustained input and output behavior instead of treating one gauge frame as an exact
measurement. If opening the valve does not restore flow, inspect upstream pressure and pump direction before raising the
limit again.

## Buffers and head-lift reset

SF+ uses a 400 m³ Fluid Buffer and a 2,400 m³ Industrial Fluid Buffer. Liquids need 8 m and 12 m of head lift
respectively to fill them completely.

A buffer does not forward head lift from an upstream machine or pump to its outlet until it is full.

```text
machine or pump -> buffer filling -> buffer full -> head lift reaches outlet
                         |
                         +-> before full, downstream pressure can remain low
```

This startup phase can look like a broken output pipe. Supply enough upstream lift to reach the top of the buffer. Once
full, treat its outlet as the beginning of the next pressure section and add another pump where that section needs one.

A buffer only delays a blockage. If a machine keeps producing a byproduct with no consumer or disposal route, the buffer
eventually fills and the machine stops.

## AWESOME Storage

SF+ progression includes AWESOME Storage Mk.1 and Mk.2. They behave like storage containers with conveyor input and
output, plus one powered sink slot. Mk.1 has 24 slots and Mk.2 has 48.

Only the final slot, shown at bottom right, feeds the AWESOME Sink. Other slots store items normally.

```text
item enters bottom-right slot
          |
          v
building powered and item sinkable? -- no --> item remains
          |
         yes
          |
          v
AWESOME points awarded -> eligible stack removed
```

Default behavior drains the whole eligible stack. An item rejected by the AWESOME Sink remains in the slot. Losing power
pauses sinking without deleting the stack. Restoring power retries the slot. The building also checks that slot when it
loads, so a supported stack saved there can be sunk after the world starts and power is available.

The host performs sinking in multiplayer. Once a supported item reaches the designated slot while powered, points are
awarded and the item is removed without a confirmation step. Treat that slot as destructive even when filling the
container through a conveyor.

### Upgrading storage

AWESOME Storage supports an upgrade build mode. Old and new storage must use the same inventory width and height, and
they must be different storage types. The upgrade keeps items only within the new container's normal slot count; it does
not expand that container to inherit a larger inventory.

Use ordinary dismantle and rebuild when placement preview rejects a container with different dimensions. Empty any slots
that would not fit before replacing a storage building through another route.

## Conveyors and transport

SF+ changes many construction recipes for belts, lifts, splitters, mergers, vehicles, trains, drones, and elevators.
Their ordinary routing, schedules, driving, and pathing use familiar game behavior. See the
[wiki catalogs](https://k-mods.com/wiki) for variants and current costs.

Fluid Extras provides fixed bends and junction shapes. Their connected ports use the same pressure and pipe-limit rules
described above. Fork Splitter and Fork Merger are directional, so their placement orientation matters.

## Main Portal startup and restart

The Main Portal links to a configured Satellite Portal and relies on its normal operating supply. SF+ changes the
startup cycle to 25 seconds. Starting from idle requires at least two fuel items. An abrupt stop also requires two fuel
items before the portal can start again.

After the link disconnects, the Main Portal has a five-second cooldown before it can reconnect. Keep spare fuel
available if an interruption would strand the route.

## Upcoming: Pressure Trigger Valve

Pressure Trigger Valve (called Pressure Regulator Valve in the development changelog) is planned for the next major SF+
release. Development code and assets exist, but the building is not part of the released catalog yet.

It is a passive directional attachment. It consumes no power, adds no head lift, and cannot force flow against network
pressure. Instead, it opens and closes from the fullness of one neighboring pipe.

Slider order selects its mode:

| Threshold order | Monitored side       | Opens when                  | Closes when                  | Between thresholds  |
| --------------- | -------------------- | --------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------- |
| `OPEN >= CLOSE` | Consumer-side Pipe A | Fill is greater than `OPEN` | Fill is less than `CLOSE`    | Keeps current state |
| `OPEN < CLOSE`  | Producer-side Pipe B | Fill is less than `OPEN`    | Fill is greater than `CLOSE` | Keeps current state |

High-fill mode works as overflow control. The valve waits for the monitored side to exceed `OPEN`, then stays open until
fullness falls below `CLOSE`.

Low-fill mode works as refill control. The valve opens when the monitored side falls below `OPEN`, then stays open until
it rises above `CLOSE`.

The band between thresholds is hysteresis. It stops small solver fluctuations from repeatedly toggling the valve.
Comparisons are strict. When both values are equal, the valve uses high-fill mode, opens only above that value, closes
only below it, and keeps its current state at the exact threshold.

A disconnected or unreadable monitored pipe closes the valve. Fullness is clamped to 140% because a pressurized pipe can
exceed nominal volume. Player thresholds range from 1% through 125%.

Displayed fill is smoothed slightly before threshold decisions. This removes some rapid movement from solver noise,
while the threshold band provides most of the stability. Copy and paste transfers both thresholds together, preserving
the selected mode and hysteresis band.
