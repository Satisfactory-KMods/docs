The right hand column is one panel with three tabs. The summary totals the
model, the inspector edits what is selected, and messages lists everything the
solver had to say. Selecting a single node switches to the inspector and reopens
the panel if you closed it.

Collapse the panel when you want the whole canvas, and on a pointer device drag
its inner edge to resize it. On a narrow screen it sits along the bottom edge
over the canvas instead of beside it.

## Summary

The summary is built from sections. One scope applies to the whole panel, and
under it you stack as many sections as you want. Each section has its own kind
and its own filters, so the same model can be read as what it delivers, what it
costs to build, and what it draws from the grid at the same time.

Add a section with the add control at the top, and remove one from its own
header. The set of sections, the scope and the panel width are saved with the
model.

A section only shows values a compatible solve produced. Until then it says so
rather than showing stale numbers.

### Scope

Six scopes decide which nodes are counted: everything, the current container,
the current container and below, the selection, the selection and everything
connected to it, and the selection and below.

### What a section can count

Output is what the model delivers. By default that is only what leaves a port
without being carried anywhere, which is the surplus you can take out of the
factory. Its filters add the flows that stay inside: used, sunk, storage and
depot.

Input is the mirror image. By default it counts the inputs nothing in the model
supplies, which is what you have to feed in. Its filters add made, mined or
extracted, and taken from storage.

The two sink point sections value the same flows in AWESOME Sink points a
minute. Output sink points counts what actually goes into a sink. Input sink
points says what your inputs would be worth if you sank them instead of using
them.

Power reports nine figures: average net, minimum made minus maximum used,
average used, maximum used, minimum and average unboosted production, minimum
and average boosted production, and the production boost as a percentage.

Overclock totals the Power Shards and the Somersloops the model uses.

Machines counts the buildings, and adds the building limit where the game data
carries one. Costs lists the parts it takes to build everything in scope.

## Inspector

The inspector edits one node at a time. Select several and it says so rather
than guessing which one you meant.

Every node has a custom title. Beyond that the inspector shows what the node
actually has: the machine and its capacity, the clock, the production slots, a
node count, a limit, and the controls its kind needs. A storage node picks its
building and its state, a resource source its resource and purity, a modular
miner its head, boosters and attachment, a generator its fuel, a hatchery its
temperature and humidity, a FaxIT network its drives, a blueprint its count and
a Space Elevator its batches.

Ports are edited here too. Add and remove them, move them up and down, give one
a part and a limit, and align a port to the connection it feeds. A limit is an
upper bound by default, and a toggle turns it into the exact rate instead. When
nothing carries a port away, the limit is what sizes the node.

Connections belong to the port they leave. Their order is what the solver
follows when a split has to prioritize, so reordering them here is how you set
priority. The same list disconnects a single connection, adds and removes
waypoints, and pins the belt or pipeline a connection is built from.

## Messages

Messages is everything the solver reported, in one place. Each one names the
node it is about rather than an internal identifier, and clicking it selects
that node on the canvas.

A clean solve says so. Only a solve that runs long enough to be worth stopping
still interrupts the canvas itself, with a banner that can cancel it.
