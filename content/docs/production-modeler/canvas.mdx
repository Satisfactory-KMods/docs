- **Double-click empty space:** open the node palette at that position.
- **Right-click empty space:** open the same palette.
- **Left-drag empty space:** pan. **Mouse wheel:** zoom around the cursor.
- **Right-drag:** draw a selection box. **Shift** or **Control:** extend selection.
- **Middle-click:** paste the copied selection.

You can also hold **Shift** and left-drag empty space to draw a selection box.
Both selection boxes include every card they overlap, even if part of the card
extends outside the box. You do not need to enclose the whole card.

The toolbar no longer has a **+ Add** button. Palette keyboard access remains
available; see [Keyboard shortcuts](/production-modeler/shortcuts/).

Hover a palette entry for its full card. A recipe or a building shows its
description, its inputs and outputs with the rate per minute, the machine that
runs it, the cycle time, the power it draws or makes, and its tier, mod and
alternate marks. A special node explains what it is, how it is used and why it
exists. The recent chips above the catalog show the same card.

Select a node to highlight its connected wires. Right-click it for editing,
copying, selecting its chain, disconnecting, or deleting. Double-click a node
to edit it in the Inspector. On narrow screens, select it and choose **Edit**
to open the panel. Double-click a container to enter it.

The bottom-left controls show the live zoom percentage. Use **Zoom out** and
**Zoom in** to change it, or **Fit canvas to view** to frame your selection.
With nothing selected, it fits the current container. These controls also work
in read-only models.

The bottom-right **Minimap** shows nodes, connections, selected nodes, and the
visible area. Click it to move the view without changing zoom. Focus it and use
arrow keys to pan, or **Enter** / **Space** to fit the current container.
Collapse it with the header button. It starts collapsed when the canvas is narrow
or short, and shrinks to stay within the available space when opened.

Use **Arrange** or **L** to lay out the selected nodes along their connections.
Arrow keys nudge the selection; hold **Shift** for larger steps.

## The panel beside the canvas

The side panel has three tabs:

- **Summary:** totals for the model, container, or selection.
- **Inspector:** settings for the selected node or connection.
- **Messages:** calculation errors, warnings, and other diagnostics.

Selecting a node opens the Inspector on desktop. On narrow screens, use **Edit**
to open it. Selecting a diagnostic reveals its node, including nodes inside
another container. Repeated diagnostics are grouped;
copy the list when reporting a problem.

The panel can be resized or hidden. On narrow screens, open it with the summary
button and close it to return to the full canvas. The button shows when errors
or warnings are waiting.

Game-data and model-status notices sit above the canvas, not above the side
panel. A long-running solve also offers a stop action there. See
[Panels](/production-modeler/panels/) for each tab's controls.

## Connections

1. Drag from an output port to a compatible input port.
2. Drop on empty space instead to open a filtered palette.
3. Pick a compatible node to place it already connected.

Arrowheads point into the receiving port. Select a node or connection to see
its highlighted wires flow from source to target. If your system requests
reduced motion, flow and loading spinners run more slowly. Decorative node
effects stay disabled.

Ports can carry several connections. In Full mode, their order controls
priority where allocation is required. Reorder connections in the Inspector.

A port limit can be an **exact rate** or an **upper bound**. A ceiling does not
force production to reach it. Limits also work on supported non-recipe buildings,
not just ordinary recipe nodes. Click a port's rate to type a limit right in the
row. Press Enter to apply it or Escape to keep the old value. The equals or
less-than-or-equal icon next to the rate switches between an exact rate and an
upper bound.

An output the recipe only rolls for, such as the off-colour slugs of a
hatchery, wears its chance per cycle as a small badge next to its name. The
port tooltip repeats it, and notes when a pity mechanic raises the long-run
share above the single roll. The rate shown is the expected yield.

A solved connection shows an automatic estimate of the belt or pipe needed for
its rate. The badge shows when more than one line is needed. It is not a picker
for assigning a particular transport to the connection.

Copied selections use the system clipboard, so they can move between models,
tabs, or windows. Browser clipboard permissions may be required.

For an input, choose Plan automatically to create its supply chain. The wizard
starts with that part and its required per-minute amount. The chain's final
producer appears at the point where you dropped the connection, with suppliers
arranged to its left. Existing nodes, pan and zoom stay where you left them.

Keyboard focus highlights the wire itself. A focused label has its own small
outline, and broken connections keep their error colour.

Grab a connection's rate label and drag it wherever it is easiest to read. This
moves only the label, not the wire or its waypoints, and does not recalculate
the model. Labels move freely even when the connection grid is on. Their
positions are saved with the model, including desktop saves and `.ksfmd` files.

Double click a label to put it back in the middle of its connection. You can
undo or redo a move or reset. With the label focused, arrow keys move it by ten
canvas pixels, Shift plus an arrow moves it by one, and Home resets it. Escape
cancels a drag. Read-only models keep their labels in the saved positions.


## Waypoints

- Double-click a connection to add a waypoint.
- Double-right-click a waypoint to remove it.
- Double-right-click a connection to delete it.
- Move both endpoints together to move their waypoints with them.

Drag a connection's rate label to adjust its routing. Double-click the label
to reset its manual placement. Label changes can be undone and saved with the
model.

Choose curves, direct lines, or orthogonal routing in
[Settings](/production-modeler/settings/#connections-and-grids).

## Grids

The decorative background grid is not a snap target. Two working grids control
placement:

- **Building grid:** snaps nodes.
- **Connection grid:** snaps waypoints.

Each has independent X and Y spacing. Apply a grid to the whole model, current
existing factory, or selection. Nudging follows the building grid when it is enabled.
Review the scope before applying a grid to existing work.

## Node status and quick controls

The **Fixed building count** field in a node header sets exactly that many
buildings running at the configured clock. It is not an item rate or a count of
installed buildings allowed to idle. Clear it to return to **Auto**. Recipe
nodes take it, and so do extractors and Modular Miners, where it says how many
of them stand on the node.

A fixed count is a plan, so what it produces is meant to be used: the chain
behind it grows to consume it, including source counts drawn as a single
building, rather than leaving that production unused. Connected
upstream limits still apply. If a fixed count needs more than its supply can
provide, the whole connected group shows **N/A** until the conflict is resolved.
The error reports the requested buildings and the reachable building-equivalents,
not items per minute. Increase the upstream supply, allow an extractor's count
to grow, or lower the fixed count or clock. Changing the downstream node to
**Auto** does not override a fixed upstream ceiling.

Use the checkmark at the bottom of a node to mark it **Built**. This tracks what
you have built in the game and is kept with the saved model. Built cards have a
uniform green outline and glow, with no extra stripe along the top. When both
ends of a connection are built, the line turns green and its rate label gains a
matching highlight. Clearing **Built** on either end restores the normal connection
styling. Diagnostic icons remain visible on built connections.

The lock beside it protects a node from moving, editing, connecting,
disconnecting, or deleting. You can still select a locked node and inspect its
details. Unlock it before making changes; its lock state is also saved.

Machine nodes keep their quick controls in a compact bottom section, visible
even when recipe details are collapsed:

- **Power:** a pill showing the calculated power use or production.
- **Buildings and clock:** a pill showing the calculated machine count and
  per-building clock percentage, such as **1 × 20%**. Use its copy button to copy
  just the percentage number for the game's clock input. The copied value has
  up to six decimal places, without a `%` sign, even if the card displays a
  rounded value or fraction. Copying also works on locked nodes and read-only
  models.
- **Clock setting:** edit the percentage beside the Power Shard icon to change
  the configured clock. This can differ from the calculated per-building clock.
- **Somersloops:** click the pill to add one, up to the machine's slot limit.
  Clicking again at the limit resets it to zero.

Available controls depend on the building. The
[Inspector](/production-modeler/panels/#inspector) keeps the detailed settings.

## Existing factories

An **Existing Factory** groups part of a factory, for example one smelting site
of a larger plan or a section you have already built. Double-click to enter;
press **Escape** or use the **Leave** button in the top left corner of the
canvas to get back out. Each container keeps its own pan and zoom.
**Select All** affects the container you are currently viewing.

Set a **count** to scale a repeated section instead of drawing the same
sub-factory repeatedly. Give it a **title** and pick an **icon** from the item
and building images in the Inspector so it reads as the site it stands for.

An existing factory does not need anything inside it. Leave it empty and set a
rate on each of its ports, on the canvas or in the Inspector: that is what one
such factory takes in and hands out per minute, so there is no ceiling or exact
to choose. The **count** in the card's footer chip is where the plan starts:
the calculation grows it when it needs more factories, the way it grows an
unfixed miner, and the chip shows the calculated count after an arrow when
they differ. With nothing pulling on the factory it stays at the count you
drew. The factory's own
**limit** caps the count. Once machines stand inside, the same port rates are
limits on the boundary again and the count is yours.

What machines inside deliver to a boundary port with nothing wired outside
leaves the model, like any unused output; a rate pinned on that port sizes
them. Wired on both sides, the pin reads once across the boundary.

An existing factory can also state its **power**: enter the MW it consumes or
generates and the summary counts that alongside the machines in the model.
Machines placed inside the container are still counted on their own, so the
stated MW is for what is not drawn.

Older models that used an outpost or a blueprint open as existing factories
with everything in place; only the inputs move ahead of the outputs.

### Wiring a container

A container's ports are the same ports on both sides. Inputs always come
before outputs; a new input slots in ahead of the outputs. Inside the
container each port stands as a node of its own, the way a model's inputs and
outputs do, so you place it where its belt arrives or leaves instead of
reading every port off one card:

1. On the overview, connect a supply to the container's **input** (for example
   Iron Ore into a smelting factory). Add ports in the inspector as needed.
2. Enter the container. That input is a **From outside** node: drag from it
   into your first machines, the ore is available there.
3. Connect your last machines to a **To outside** node, the container's
   output.
4. Back on the overview, the container's **output** feeds the rest of the plan.

Inside, the palette offers **Factory input** and **Factory output**: placing
one adds the port to the container and stands it where you dropped it, and
dragging a connection onto empty canvas offers the matching one already wired.
Drag a port node where it reads best; delete it, or use the bin in its header,
to remove the port from the container. Adding a port in the Inspector puts a
new node inside in the same way. Inputs start in a column on the left and
outputs on the right until you move them.

Nothing needs to be typed twice: an input takes its part from the belt that
reaches it outside and hands the same part on inside. The count multiplies
what its machines make, so its ports show the total that crosses; a counted
factory's port shows that total first and the rate one factory states
beneath it.

The header trail shows where you are. Use it to return to a parent container.
A closed container shows a summary without requiring you to enter it.

## Reusing a model in another model

A model can stand in another model as one node, a **Sub-model**, and scale
like a recipe. First say what the model offers:

1. In the model you want to reuse, place **Model Input** nodes for the parts
   it takes from outside and **Model Output** nodes for the parts it delivers.
   Each carries one part; connect them to the machines that use or make it.
   Several nodes of the same part pool into one entry.
2. Solve the model as usual. The **Sub-models** tab in the panel shows the
   interface that results: each part with the rate one copy of the model
   moves, and the power one copy draws and makes.

Then, in the plan that uses it, choose **Sub-model (Exp)** in the palette and pick
the model from the catalog: your own models, the ones saved on this device, or
a public one. Each card shows what the model delivers and needs, its tags and
folder; hover it for the long card with size, buildings and revision. Search
by name, tag, folder or product, filter by where a model lives, and hide the
ones without an interface with **Placeable only**. Type a name and press Enter
to place the first match.
The model is solved on its own once and placed as a node whose ports carry
those rates. Ask for 60 plates from a model that makes 30 and the calculation
counts two copies; the inputs scale with them, and so does the power. Whole
copies are the default; the Inspector offers the fraction, a copy limit as a
ceiling or an exact count. A sub-model cannot be overclocked or slooped. It
can be locked and marked as built like any other node.

**Open model** in the node's header, in its context menu or in the Inspector shows the
model it stands for: a new browser tab, or on the desktop the same window with
a way back to the plan in the header. The desktop saves your plan first; if
that save fails, you stay on the plan and Messages says why. **Refresh** reads
the model again. When
a plan opens, every placed model is compared with the model it stands for and
brought up to date if that model changed; Messages lists what was refreshed
and what could not be read. Ports of parts that stay keep their connections.

A model cannot be placed inside itself, directly or through another model.

## Aligning a port

Choose **Align to connection** in the Inspector to move the node vertically
until its port lines up with the connected port. Horizontal placement stays
unchanged. If the port has several connections, the first one is used.
