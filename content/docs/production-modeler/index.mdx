The Production Modeler is a drawing board for factories. You place the buildings
you want, connect them the way the belts run, and the modeler works out the
rates. Nothing is chosen for you.

:::caution[Alpha]
The modeler is still in alpha. Models you save are kept, but the catalog behind
them changes as the game data does, and features are still landing.
:::

## Modeler or Planner

The [Production Planner](/production-planner/) answers a different question. You
tell it what you want per minute, it picks recipes and buildings and hands you a
finished plan.

The modeler starts from the other end. You already know roughly what you want to
build, and you want to see whether it adds up. Nothing is auto-selected, every
node is yours, and the solver only fills in the numbers that follow from what
you placed.

Pick the planner when you want a plan. Pick the modeler when you want to model
something you have in mind, including layouts the planner would never generate.

## Your first model

Open the modeler and create a model. The canvas starts empty.

Double click empty space to open the palette, or right click for the same menu.
Search by recipe name, by input, or by output, and toggle those three fields
individually. Typing several words matches all of them; if that finds nothing,
the palette falls back to matching any of them and says so.

Open the filters and you can narrow the results without typing. Pick one or more
mods to see only their recipes, pick a machine tier, pick the progression tier
that unlocks a recipe, and switch the alternate control to see only alternate
recipes or none of them. Nothing is selected by default, so the palette still
starts with everything the data set contains. The ten special nodes ignore these
controls, because a sink or a storage belongs to no mod and no tier.

The palette is a small panel you can drag out of the way, and it leaves the
canvas visible and usable underneath. It opens with the search field ready, so
you can type straight away. Results are a grid of tiles; the arrow keys move
through them in both directions and Enter places the highlighted one.

The filters sit behind their own toggle, which carries a count while any of
them is on, and every time the palette opens it starts from nothing so a filter
from last time cannot hide what you are looking for.

Pick an entry and it lands where you clicked. Drag from a port and drop on empty
space to open the palette already filtered to what fits that port, then the new
node arrives connected.

Press the question mark key or F1 at any time for the full list of shortcuts.

## Reading a node

A node stays compact: its name, its result, and one row per port. Inputs sit on
the left, outputs on the right, and every row names the part it carries and how
much of it moves. Nothing floats over anything else, so a node stays readable
however many ports it has.

The chevron in the header opens the node. That is where the clock, the
production slots, an optional limit and the machine choice live, and everything
there is also available in the inspector while the node stays closed.

Solids are counted in items per minute, fluids and gases in cubic metres per
minute, and every value says so. That is the unit the game shows you, so a pipe
carrying 300 m³ reads as 300 and not as 300000.

A recipe node shows the rate it produces. Open it and switch off the per minute
rates to see how many machines that takes instead.

Values are exact fractions internally, so a chain of thirds stays a third
instead of drifting into decimals. The formatted number is what you see, the
exact value sits in the tooltip.

A node that cannot solve is marked, and the marker says why. You do not have to
hunt through a global list to find out which node broke.

## What the solver does

Four modes decide how much the solver is allowed to conclude. None leaves every
value blank while you arrange things. Manual solves the exact equalities you
entered. Basic adds limits and maximizes what flows. Full also honours the order
of your connections when a split has to prioritize.

The solver runs on its own after a change, and a long run can be stopped. Values
it cannot determine stay unknown rather than being guessed.

## Special buildings

Beside the recipes the palette carries the nodes that are not recipes: a
splitter and a merger for routing, an outpost and a blueprint for grouping, and
the sink, storage, depot and space elevator. Splitter and merger are the same
free node in two shapes, and you can add further ports to either. Which
connection is served first is the order of the connections on the port, so there
is no separate priority node to choose.

Satisfactory Plus buildings that do not behave like an ordinary recipe get their
own treatment. Refined Power boilers require both their boiler and their heater,
a Slug Hatchery only produces inside its comfortable temperature and humidity, a
farming plot runs faster with a greenhouse module, and an Air Collector produces
from what it can reach.

Where the game data does not describe something completely, the modeler says so
instead of inventing a number. A turbine moves its steam correctly, but the
electricity a generator makes from it depends on values the game data never
ships, so the modeler reports that rather than showing zero.
